California is one of the most worker-protective states in the country. That protection only matters if someone enforces it.

California's Labor Code, the Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA), and federal statutes like Title VII, the ADA, and the FMLA give workers substantial rights against wrongful conduct at work. Enforcement of those rights is what employment lawyers do. The Ramirez Firm represents California employees — never employers — across the full range of workplace claims.

What we handle in these cases.

Employment cases fall into several overlapping categories. Most clients come in believing they have one claim, and end up with two or three once the file is reviewed.

  • Wrongful termination (public policy, whistleblower, retaliation)
  • Discrimination (age, race, gender, disability, pregnancy, religion, national origin)
  • Harassment (sexual, hostile work environment)
  • Retaliation for reporting harassment, discrimination, wage theft, or safety issues
  • Wage and overtime violations (misclassification, unpaid overtime, meal and rest break violations)
  • FMLA and CFRA leave violations
  • Severance package review and negotiation
  • Independent contractor misclassification (AB 5 / ABC test)

What California law says.

California employment law is largely codified in the Labor Code, the Fair Employment and Housing Act (Gov. Code § 12900 et seq.), and Industrial Welfare Commission (IWC) wage orders. Deadlines are strict — many claims must be filed with the California Civil Rights Department (formerly DFEH) within three years of the wrongful act, and other claims carry two-year or one-year statutes of limitations depending on the claim type.

If this sounds like your situation

If you were fired, disciplined, denied leave, or paid less than you should have been in the last 12 months, and you suspect something wrong is going on — call. The consultation is free and it costs nothing to find out whether the case is worth pursuing.

How the case actually gets built.

Every case John accepts follows the same structural approach. Preserve the evidence early. Interview witnesses while memory is fresh. Get the documentary record — HR file, medical records, police reports, ELD data, surveillance video — under a preservation letter before it disappears. Frame the case for trial from day one, even if it settles at mediation months later.

On the employment side, we build the case around the timeline and the paper trail. When did the client complain, and when did discipline appear? What does the personnel file actually say, and does the metadata match the story? Are there text messages, emails, or Slack messages that corroborate what the client remembers? These are the mechanics that turn a he-said-she-said dispute into a case that resolves in the client's favor.

Fees and timeline.

Employment cases at the firm are handled on contingency: no fee unless we recover for you. Case timelines vary. Some cases settle at mediation within four to six months of the demand letter. Others go through discovery and settle before trial. Some try. During the free consultation, John will give you an honest read on which category yours is likely to fall into.

California gives workers real rights. Whether those rights matter to you personally depends on whether you have someone who knows how to enforce them.
