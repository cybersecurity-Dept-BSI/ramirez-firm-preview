California unpaid wages claims come with statutory penalties on top of the wages themselves.

Beyond the wages owed, California law imposes waiting-time penalties (up to 30 days of wages) for late final paychecks under Labor Code § 203, wage statement penalties under § 226, and PAGA penalties on a per-pay-period basis.

What we handle in these cases.

When unpaid wages cases come into the firm, they usually break down into a few recurring patterns.

  • Case intake and initial evaluation
  • Evidence preservation and demand letter
  • Negotiation, mediation, or arbitration
  • Litigation and trial preparation when needed

What California law says.

California statutes, applicable Labor Code sections, and controlling case law govern each of these claims. Deadlines vary. Free consultation covers the specific statute of limitations for your situation.

If this sounds like your situation

If you left a job in the last 12 months and did not receive your final paycheck within the required window, penalties may be accruing.

How the case actually gets built.

Every case John accepts follows the same structural approach. Preserve the evidence early. Interview witnesses while memory is fresh. Get the documentary record — HR file, medical records, police reports, ELD data, surveillance video — under a preservation letter before it disappears. Frame the case for trial from day one, even if it settles at mediation months later.

Unpaid Wages cases at the firm follow the same discipline that runs through every John Ramirez case: preserve evidence early, build the case around the documentary record, and prepare for trial even if the case will settle. Trial preparation is what generates leverage at mediation.
