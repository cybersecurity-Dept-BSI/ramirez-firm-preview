Criminal defense is not paperwork. It is trial preparation from day one, whether the case ever sees a jury or not.

John Ramirez spent seven years as a Deputy District Attorney in Alameda, Kern, and San Bernardino Counties. He ran 72 felony jury trials before switching to defense-side practice. Today he brings that trial experience — and the insider knowledge of how the DA's office builds a case — to criminal defense clients.

What we handle in these cases.

The firm handles the full range of California criminal defense matters, from misdemeanors through serious and violent felonies.

  • DUI (VC § 23152) and DUI-related charges
  • Felony offenses (violent crimes, robbery, assault with a deadly weapon)
  • Misdemeanor offenses (petty theft, disorderly conduct, minor drug offenses)
  • Domestic violence (PC § 273.5, PC § 243(e)(1))
  • Drug offenses (possession, sales, transportation — HS Code violations)
  • Weapons offenses (PC § 25850, PC § 29800)
  • Post-conviction relief and expungement (PC § 1203.4, PC § 1170.18)

What California law says.

California Penal Code, Vehicle Code, and Health and Safety Code govern criminal charges. Constitutional protections under the Fourth, Fifth, Sixth, and Fourteenth Amendments — as interpreted by California appellate courts — control what evidence can be used and what procedures must be followed. Deadlines for motions, discovery, and plea negotiations are set by the assigned court.

If this sounds like your situation

If you have been arrested, do not talk to law enforcement about the case without an attorney present. Do not consent to searches you can decline. Call before the arraignment when possible — a lot of case value is decided in the first 48 hours.

How the case actually gets built.

Every case John accepts follows the same structural approach. Preserve the evidence early. Interview witnesses while memory is fresh. Get the documentary record — HR file, medical records, police reports, ELD data, surveillance video — under a preservation letter before it disappears. Frame the case for trial from day one, even if it settles at mediation months later.

Criminal defense at the firm starts with the discovery review — police reports, body-worn camera, witness statements, forensic reports. John looks for what a defense-side attorney with prosecutor experience would flag: chain-of-custody problems, suppressible statements, constitutional violations that support motions to suppress. From there, the case runs on parallel tracks: negotiate the best resolution available while preparing the case for trial. Trial preparation is what generates leverage in negotiation.
